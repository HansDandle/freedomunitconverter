The ultimate freedom unit: hours of your life

How many hours of work does it take to buy…?

Price ÷ hourly pay, measured three ways: the federal minimum wage, the average worker's hourly wage, and median household income spread over a 2,080-hour work year (40 hrs × 52 weeks). Each panel has its own scale. Click a legend item to hide or show that line.

  • Minimum wage: federal minimum in effect for most of that year (many states now set higher minimums; 26 states were above $7.25 in 2025).
  • Average worker: BLS average hourly earnings, production & nonsupervisory employees, total private (annual average; 2025 ≈ estimate).
  • Household: Census median household income (nominal) ÷ 2,080. Many households have two earners, so this is "household dollars per full-time hour," not one person's wage. 2025 is an estimate.
  • Gold: annual average price per troy ounce. House: Census median sales price of new homes sold, used as a stand-in for a typical 3-bedroom house.
  • Groceries, gas, electricity: BLS average retail prices (milk before 1995 from USDA retail data). New car: average new-vehicle transaction price.
  • Rent: Census median gross rent (decennial census + ACS; 1975, 1985, 1995 interpolated). College: College Board / NCES published in-state tuition & fees. Health insurance: KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey, family coverage, total premium; the survey begins in 1999.
  • Movie ticket: National Association of Theatre Owners average. Disney: published one-day Magic Kingdom gate prices (2020+ uses a typical date-based price). Rolex: approximate U.S. list prices for the no-date Submariner. NFL: Team Marketing Report average from 1991; earlier years estimated.
  • Cigarettes: average retail price per pack including federal and state taxes (Tax Burden on Tobacco / CDC). Stamps: USPS first-class letter rate in effect for most of the year.
  • Items marked "Estimate" (tires, birth, tampons, condoms, toilet paper, haircut, beer, flight, dinner, NFL before 1991, computer, phone call, TV) have no single official series; values are representative figures and should be read as rough.
  • TV: no official series exists; these are representative prices for a typical mainstream family TV of the era (19–25" color CRT through 2000, ~32" LCD in 2005, 50–55" LED/4K after). Rough estimates.
  • All figures are nominal dollars rounded from published series; recent years (esp. 2025) are approximate. Pay is pre-tax.
